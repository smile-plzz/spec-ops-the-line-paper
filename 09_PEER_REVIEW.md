## 2. Individual Reviewer Reports

### Seat 1: Journal-Fit Reviewer
- **Overall Impression:** This manuscript is an extraordinarily thorough, intellectually ambitious, and well-researched study of *Spec Ops: The Line*. It offers a compelling synthesis of procedural rhetoric, ethics, and postcolonial critique.
- **Critical Concern 1 (Word Count Excess):** The manuscript's body text (Sections 1–13) currently stands at approximately 12,600 words. For *Game Studies* and *Games and Culture*, typical submission guidelines stipulate an upper limit of 8,000 to 9,000 words. The author must execute a disciplined condensation, trimming approximately 3,500–4,000 words without sacrificing conceptual depth or key citations.
- **Critical Concern 2 (Title & Framing):** The introduction is somewhat repetitive in establishing the historical context of modern military shooters. Condense the introductory rhetoric and foreground the primary thesis earlier.
- **Recommendation:** Major Revision (conditioned strictly on length reduction and structural tightening).

---

### Seat 2: Reviewer 1 (Methodology & Textual Analysis)
- **Strengths:** The Scene Analysis Matrix (Appendix A / file `05_SCENE_ANALYSIS_MATRIX.md`) and the Character Ecology Matrix are exemplary. The author demonstrates granular, scene-level familiarity with the game's mechanics, audio cues, cutscenes, and branching micro-choices.
- **Weakness:** In Section 8, the mechanical discussion of ammunition scarcity and cover-snapping is insightful, but the paper could more explicitly cite the specific controller inputs (e.g., the dual-use of the sprint/cover button, the squad focus command button) to anchor the embodied "muscle memory" argument.
- **Correction:** Clarify the distinction between diegetic sound and non-diegetic soundtrack in the Radioman analysis.
- **Recommendation:** Minor Revision.

---

### Seat 3: Reviewer 2 (Game Ethics & Procedural Rhetoric)
- **Strengths:** The concept of **Performative Compliance** is an original and valuable theoretical contribution. It effectively breaks the simplistic binary between Sicart's autonomous ethical agency and the cynical dismissal of the game as a manipulative railroad.
- **Challenge:** The author must be careful not to make excuses for Yager Development. When Walt Williams claims that "the player could always just turn off the game," is this truly a profound meta-ludic choice, or is it an *ex post facto* rationalization for linear game design that couldn't afford branching narrative paths? The manuscript needs to address this counter-reading directly.
- **Recommendation:** Major Revision.

---

### Seat 4: Reviewer 3 (Visual Culture & Postcolonial Media Studies)
- **Strengths:** The inclusion of Soraya Murray's critique and Edward Said's Orientalism is vital and elevates the paper beyond standard, fawning game studies retrospectives. The discussion of the voiceless Arab civilian and the spatial "zone of exception" (Gregory) is sharply articulated.
- **Suggestion:** Strengthen the intersectional analysis of gender in Section 11. Specifically, note how the charred mother and child in Chapter 8 function as a secularized, desecrated *Pietà* (drawing on Bosman, 2025/2026), demonstrating how Western art history tropes are imported to sanctify the white soldier's grief.
- **Recommendation:** Minor Revision.

---

### Seat 5: Reviewer 4 (The Devil's Advocate)
- **Core Challenge to the Thesis:** The author's central thesis—that *Spec Ops: The Line* successfully weaponizes player compliance to expose the ethics of the shooter genre—gives the game far too much credit.
  1. *The Hypocrisy of Militainment:* If 95% of the player's time is spent having fun shooting enemies in the head, no amount of accusatory loading screens can undo the fact that the player was entertained by slaughter. The game is fundamentally complicit in the very economy it critiques.
  2. *The Fallacy of 'Walking Away':* The assertion that turning off the console is a valid moral choice is philosophically incoherent. When a consumer purchases a narrative software product, their participation is governed by the contract of consumption. Turning off the game is not an ethical decision within the fictional world; it is the abandonment of the aesthetic experience. To blame the player for finishing a game they paid for is fundamentally bad-faith design.
  3. *Walker vs. Player:* The paper at times conflates Walker's guilt with the player's guilt. Walker is a fictional character in a story who made catastrophic tactical mistakes; the player is a human sitting on a couch operating a computer program. The paper must maintain this ontological boundary with absolute rigor.
- **Recommendation:** Major Revision (demanding substantial philosophical pushback and rigorous qualification of claims).
